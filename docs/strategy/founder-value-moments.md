# MasterMind — Founder Value Moments

> **Status (2026-07-28):** Living evidence log. Three moments recorded, from Erik's
> Vallaki development work (Curse of Strahd adaptation). Structure developed
> iteratively by Erik with ChatGPT and Claude review.
>
> **Boundary:** This document records and analyzes founder value moments. Decisions
> about messaging, demonstrations, launch assets, and outward claims are made
> elsewhere.
>
> **Content note:** entries contain Curse of Strahd spoilers and reference WotC's
> published material — internal document.

## Governing instruction

**Record every meaningful value moment, including unresolved ones. Classify without
forcing. Synthesize patterns only as the evidence accumulates.**

This log must accept unfinished observations, remain open to new *kinds* of value, and
support pattern discovery over time. A moment does not need a final creative decision to
count as evidence — value that arrives mid-process (surfacing options, revealing
constraints, prompting questions) is evidence about an earlier part of the value chain,
not incomplete evidence. New examples must never be forced into the first categories we
happened to invent; the taxonomy below evolves as entries accumulate.

The long-term goal: as entries accumulate and recur, let repeated patterns **earn**
promotion into candidate value propositions (no quota — capture broadly, tag
consistently, promote what recurs).

## Evidence rules

1. **Founder evidence is not customer evidence.** These are observations from one
   founder and one campaign. Classifications: founder observation / customer interview /
   beta-user report / verified behavior / research-supported mechanism. Counts show
   *recurrence, not proof*. A pattern becomes more strategically credible when it
   appears across different tasks, different parts of the campaign, different
   campaigns, in beta-user reports, in behavior users can describe or demonstrate, and
   in material that ultimately reaches play.
2. **Observed value is kept separate from anticipated value.** Each entry
   distinguishes: *immediate value observed* (what changed in thinking or campaign
   development), *creative output produced* (what was decided, added, or revised),
   *anticipated playable value* (how it might affect preparation or play), and
   *realized table value* (what actually happened after players encountered it). The
   log's credibility depends on never quietly converting "this gave me a promising
   idea" into "this improved the campaign."
3. **Source facts are kept separate from founder inference.** The value mechanism runs:
   source fact → relationship represented on the map → question or inference surfaced →
   founder's creative decision. Entries label which layer each statement belongs to
   rather than calling the whole chain published fact.
4. **Quotations carry provenance.** Every quotation is labeled as one of: **verbatim
   founder statement** (with date/transcript reference), **edited founder statement**
   (original meaning preserved), or **working synthesis** (created from the observation,
   awaiting founder adoption). A polished line written collaboratively must never
   silently become a verbatim founder quote.
5. **Costs and counterevidence are recorded too.** A value log that records only
   successes becomes a confirmation-bias machine. For substantial moments, capture: how
   much mapping preceded the insight; whether entering/maintaining the graph felt
   burdensome; whether the insight might have arisen from rereading or sustained
   attention alone; whether the idea was later abandoned; whether the map ever created
   confusion or suggested an unhelpful connection; whether the resulting material
   reached play. What's eventually needed is the value-to-effort relationship, not just
   the value.

## Maturity states and dispositions

A moment is recordable at any stage; state is updated as it progresses. Progression is
not assumed — non-success outcomes are valid dispositions, and a surfaced option that is
later rejected may still demonstrate option-generation value (it simply must not appear
to be advancing toward play).

| Progression states | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Surfaced** | The graph raised a question, connection, or option. |
| **Developing** | Comparing possibilities or working toward a decision. |
| **Decided** | Chose how the insight will affect the campaign. |
| **Readied** | Translated into material usable during play. |
| **Played** | It reached the table. |
| **Reflected** | Observed what effect it had during play. |

| Dispositions | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Parked** | Set aside intentionally; may resume. |
| **Rejected** | Considered and declined. |
| **Superseded** | Replaced by a different insight or approach. |
| **Abandoned** | Dropped without a deliberate decision. |
| **No longer relevant** | The campaign moved past it. |

## Tag dimensions

Every enriched entry is tagged on three separate dimensions. One observation can carry
several tags per dimension. These starter lists grow as new kinds of value appear.

- **Workflow** (what was the GM doing?): worldbuilding · published-adventure adaptation
  · character development · faction development · session preparation · re-immersion ·
  improvisation · at-table reference
- **Apparent mechanism** (what did the graph make easier?): cueing existing knowledge ·
  seeing related facts together · tracing a causal chain · scanning a character or
  location's neighborhood · generating options · comparing alternatives · identifying
  constraints · finding missing connective tissue · recognizing contradictions ·
  following consequences
- **Value produced** (what did that enable?): richer character motivation · stronger
  world coherence · more credible consequences · better integration of new ideas · more
  playable hooks · more preparation options · faster re-immersion · greater preparation
  confidence · more relevant context during improvisation

## Capture workflow — two stages

Logging must not feel like a chore (the same cardinal principle as the product itself).

**Stage 1 — Quick capture** (at the moment, minutes not hours):

- Date and context
- What I was trying to do
- What the map made me notice
- What changed in my thinking
- Current state
- Supporting artifact if available (screenshot of the graph neighborhood, voice note,
  source transcript)

**Stage 2 — Evidence enrichment** (later, for entries worth the analysis): mechanism
and value tags · source facts vs. founder inferences · narrow claim supported ·
alternative explanations · observed vs. anticipated value split · costs/counterevidence
· follow-up.

**Supporting artifacts (optional but valuable):** screenshot of the relevant graph
neighborhood · workspace and node names · product version or date · before-and-after
notes · original voice transcript · links to resulting campaign material. Without
artifacts, future analysis depends on reconstructed memory and risks hindsight bias;
artifacts show exactly what was visible when the insight occurred.

## Value Moment 1 — The baroness as a conflicted character

**State:** Developing (leaning: play the baroness as a conflicted potential weak link in
the burgomaster's position — "very likely," not final).
**Workflow tags:** published-adventure adaptation · character development.
**Product interaction:** not precisely recorded — the moment arose during/after mapping
Vallaki's characters, locations, factions, and relationships (encoding relationships and
seeing the resulting cluster together). Capture the exact preceding interaction in
future entries.

**Date / context:** 2026-07-25 weekend, developing the town of Vallaki for a Curse of
Strahd adaptation. Erik mapped its major characters, locations, factions, and
relationships. He had read this section of the published adventure **at least three
times.**

**Source material** (as summarized by Erik from the published module):

- The burgomaster is married to the priest's sister.
- The burgomaster has imprisoned members of the priest's congregation for opposing his
  rule and his vision.
- The burgomaster's wife (the baroness) benefits from her position — she lives a very
  good life.

**Questions / inferences surfaced by seeing the relationships together** (founder
inference layer — these are not module facts): across three linear reads, the facts
above registered separately — "okay, there's a relationship there" — and washed over
each time. Mapped together, the family, political, and religious relationships raised:

- How does the priest feel about his brother-in-law imprisoning members of his
  congregation? (Inference: he must have feelings about it.)
- How does the baroness reconcile loyalty to her husband with concern for her brother
  and his congregation? (Inference: she must be conflicted.)
- Does she genuinely support the burgomaster, or must she publicly perform that support?
- Could her divided loyalty make her a vulnerability in the burgomaster's position?

**Immediate value observed:** previously-inert known facts combined into a recognized
emotional and political implication; the baroness changed in Erik's understanding from a
supporting character defined by her position into a potentially conflicted person with
competing loyalties. Erik's own assessment (edited founder statement, from the
2026-07-28 account): without the map these characters would have been played "very
monotone" in their motivations.

**Creative output produced:** a direction, not yet final — "very likely" play the
baroness as one of the burgomaster's weak links: someone who might be swayed because
deep down she may not agree with him, while wearing the persona of the baroness who
does. The priest–burgomaster relationship also gained emotional and political weight in
Erik's read of the town.

**Anticipated playable value** (not yet observed): more nuanced roleplaying; a
relationship the players can investigate or influence; a believable source of
information or assistance; internal conflict within the ruling family; consequences that
emerge from established relationships rather than an arbitrary plot device.

**Realized table value:** none yet — has not reached play.

**Apparent mechanism tags:** cueing existing knowledge · seeing related facts together ·
finding missing connective tissue.
**Value tags:** richer character motivation · more playable hooks · stronger world
coherence.

**Evidence classification:** founder observation.

**Provisional claim supported:** by keeping a character's relationships, environment,
and history visible together, MasterMind may help a GM develop motivations and tensions
that are easier to overlook when those facts appear on separate pages.

**What this does not prove:** that every GM would miss these implications without
MasterMind; that MasterMind originated the underlying facts; that every relationship
placed on a graph will produce a useful insight; that another GM could not have reached
the same interpretation through ordinary notes; that the insight owes nothing to a
fourth pass through the material.

**Costs / counterevidence:** not yet recorded — capture with the next update (how much
Vallaki mapping preceded the insight; whether entry felt burdensome).

**Follow-up:** does the baroness direction get finalized, readied, and played? Do
players discover or exploit the vulnerability?

## Value Moment 2 — The bones as a campaign-wide catalyst

**State:** Decided (reframe adopted: the bones are a catalyst for conflict between
Vallaki and Castle Ravenloft, not merely local church drama); not yet readied for play.
**Workflow tags:** published-adventure adaptation · worldbuilding.
**Product interaction:** not precisely recorded — the insight arose from the mapped
chain (object, characters, motives, locations, relationships) being visible together;
the exact preceding interaction was not captured.

**Date / context:** same Vallaki mapping session. The bones of St. Andral — buried under
the church altar, making it hallowed ground — initially registered across multiple reads
as "an item that gives reason for drama within Vallaki, specifically within the church,"
to be played locally and seen where it goes.

**Source material** (as summarized by Erik from the published module):

- The priest knows the bones are why the church is protected.
- The priest shares that secret with Yeska, an orphan altar boy, to reassure him he is
  safe from the vampire threat.
- Yeska tells Milivoj, the young gravedigger and groundskeeper at the church.
- Milivoj is working to care for his brother and sister and needs money.
- The coffin maker learns about the bones and pays Milivoj to get them.
- The coffin maker is acting for a traveler, Vasili von Holtz.
- Vasili von Holtz is Strahd in disguise.

**Founder inferences layered on those facts** (interpretation, not module text): the
priest is the nearest thing Yeska has to family, giving the reassurance emotional
weight; Yeska and Milivoj plausibly have a big-brother dynamic through working together;
Milivoj's real priority is his actual siblings, which makes his choice understandable
pressure rather than betrayal for its own sake.

**Immediate value observed:** mapped, the bones stopped looking like an isolated local
quest item and appeared as a **causal bridge** — a chain connecting a frightened orphan
→ a protective priest → a young worker under financial pressure → a local tradesperson
→ Strahd's hidden influence → the safety of the church → the wider vulnerability of
Vallaki → Castle Ravenloft's reach beyond the castle. Additional questions surfaced:
what kind of relationship exists between Yeska and Milivoj; how does financial pressure
shape Milivoj's choices; what does the theft reveal about Strahd's reach into Vallaki;
which characters would feel betrayed if the full chain became known.

**Creative output produced:** the reframe was adopted — Erik now treats the bones as a
catalyst for town-versus-castle conflict rather than an item generating local church
drama.

**Anticipated playable value** (not yet observed): more believable NPC behavior; a
stronger chain of clues; emotional consequences when trust is broken; conflict
connecting church, town, and castle; several points where players can intervene; a
local event that reveals something larger about Strahd's power.

**Realized table value:** none yet — has not reached play.

**Apparent mechanism tags:** tracing a causal chain · following consequences · seeing
related facts together.
**Value tags:** more credible consequences · stronger world coherence · more playable
hooks.

**Evidence classification:** founder observation, consistent with the
connection-tracing mechanism (see mechanism note above).

**Provisional claim supported:** by making causal and relational chains visible,
MasterMind may help a GM recognize how an existing story element could create
consequences across multiple parts of the campaign.

**What this does not prove:** that MasterMind created the published relationship chain;
that every mapped item will reveal campaign-wide consequences; that another GM would
interpret or develop these relationships the same way; that the resulting material will
necessarily improve the players' experience.

**Costs / counterevidence:** not yet recorded — capture with the next update.

**Follow-up:** does the town–castle catalyst framing get readied and played? Does it
produce the intended cross-campaign conflict?

## Value Moment 3 — Integrating a reinterpreted Van Richten

**State:** Developing — no final creative decision yet. (Recorded deliberately at this
stage: the value occurred *before* a decision. The map expanded and organized the
decision space.)
**Workflow tags:** published-adventure adaptation · character development ·
worldbuilding.
**Product interaction:** scanning the mapped Vallaki neighborhood at a glance while
considering where Van Richten could fit ("at a glance, I can look at what is in
Vallaki" — near-verbatim, 2026-07-28); exact sequence otherwise not captured.

**Date / context:** 2026-07-28, continuing Vallaki development. Rudolph Van Richten is
an NPC Erik wants the players to connect with — a vampire hunter secretly operating in
Vallaki. Erik doesn't love the published depiction (an extroverted carnival ringmaster
boasting about his adventures) and wants to play him instead as a rugged fighter type
who can act as a guide and leader to the party early on.

**Goal:** reinterpret Van Richten as a rugged, capable hunter and potential early guide
while making that characterization feel naturally rooted in Vallaki rather than bolted
onto it.

**Source material** (as summarized by Erik from the published module):

- Van Richten is secretly operating in Vallaki.
- The published depiction uses the extroverted carnival-ringmaster persona.
- He is secretly gathering information on the Martikov family.
- The Martikovs run the Blue Water Inn in Vallaki, operate the Wizard of Wines winery,
  and are key members of the Keepers of the Feather faction.
- Vallaki contains two established wolf hunters.
- Fiona Wachter is a loyal servant of Strahd.

**Options and inferences surfaced by scanning the map** (founder layer):

- Van Richten could join the existing wolf hunters as their newest (third) member.
- Or he could operate as a competing hunter — a newcomer who on his own is somehow as
  effective as the two established hunters, creating tension.
- A hunting role supports the desired rugged characterization.
- His existing investigation of the Martikovs connects naturally to the town's social
  structure.
- Alignments that do *not* fit the intended interpretation were quickly visible and
  eliminated (the church; Fiona Wachter).
- A connection Erik had not started with surfaced during the scan: Van Richten should
  plausibly know about — and probably be investigating — Fiona Wachter as well.
  (Verbatim founder phrasing, 2026-07-28: "I'm realizing right now as I'm doing
  this…")

**Immediate value observed:** the graph's at-a-glance view of Vallaki's people and
factions made it easy to find integration hooks quickly — to generate and compare
coherent ways of embedding a reinterpreted character into the existing world, and to
rule out options that conflict with the intent. This is meaningfully different from
Moments 1–2: the value is **option generation and comparison**, and it arrived before
any final answer.

**Creative output produced:** none final yet. Constraint decisions were made (no church
alignment; no Wachter alignment), and a candidate option set now exists (third hunter
vs. competing hunter), plus a new investigative thread to consider (Van Richten →
Wachter).

**Anticipated playable value** (not yet observed): a characterization rooted in the
town; conflict or camaraderie with the established hunters; investigative relationships
the players can encounter from multiple angles.

**Realized table value:** none yet.

**Apparent mechanism tags:** scanning a location's neighborhood · generating options ·
comparing alternatives · identifying constraints · cueing existing knowledge
(relationship discovery: the Wachter connection).
**Value tags:** better integration of new ideas · richer characterization · more
credible relationships · additional conflict · more playable hooks · investigative
possibilities.

> "The map let me look across Vallaki and quickly test several ways Van Richten could
> belong there. It did not choose one for me, but it surfaced roles, conflicts, and
> investigative relationships I had not started with."
>
> *Provenance: working synthesis (ChatGPT, 2026-07-28), created from Erik's account —
> awaiting founder adoption.*

**Evidence classification:** founder observation.

**Provisional claim supported:** an at-a-glance view of a location's people, factions,
and relationships may help a GM generate, compare, and constrain coherent options for
integrating a new or reinterpreted element into an existing world.

**What this does not prove:** that the final interpretation will work during play; that
the surfaced options are better than the published characterization; that another GM
would generate the same options; that MasterMind measurably reduced preparation time;
that every character benefits from this type of mapping.

**Costs / counterevidence:** not yet recorded — capture with the next update.

**Follow-up:** which option is ultimately selected (record even if the answer is
"neither" — a rejected option set still demonstrates option-generation value)? What
relationships get added to the map (e.g., Van Richten → Wachter)? Is the interpretation
readied for play? Does it help portray Van Richten consistently? Do players engage with
the resulting relationships or conflicts?

## Provisional pattern register

Candidate value patterns supported so far. These are **not** validated value
propositions — they are candidates that earn promotion through recurrence (see evidence

persuasive than one pattern appearing across campaigns, workflows, and users.

### Value-strength criteria (activate when a pattern accumulates multiple entries)

Recurrence alone will not determine which patterns deserve promotion. Once a pattern has
several supporting entries, evaluate it across: **frequency** (how often it happens) ·
**breadth** (workflows, campaigns, users) · **magnitude** (slightly helped / materially
changed development / reshaped the campaign) · **proximity to play** (stayed an idea /
readied / reached the table) · **value-to-effort** (was the insight worth the mapping
required?) · **distinctiveness** (could ordinary notes provide the same benefit as
easily?) · **customer support** (do beta users report or demonstrate it?). No scoring
yet — these activate as patterns begin recurring.

## Current working synthesis from VM1–VM3 (provisional)

> "The graph did not give me new information. It changed what I could do with
> information I already had."
>
> *Provenance: working synthesis (ChatGPT, 2026-07-28), created from the Vallaki
> observations — awaiting founder adoption.*

This summarizes the current three entries only, and is deliberately placed *after* the
evidence rather than before it. Future moments may involve re-immersion, correcting an
inconsistency, reducing preparation effort, or something genuinely different — this line
must not become the lens that decides what qualifies as value.

**Mechanism note:** these moments are consistent with cueing, co-location, and
connection-tracing mechanisms (see [brandscript.md](./brandscript.md), Appendix A, for
the underlying research pass). They do not establish which mechanism caused the insight
or rule out additional effects from deliberate encoding, reflection, or time spent with
the material. MasterMind did not supply the creative answers; the visible representation
appears to have helped Erik access and reason across relevant campaign material — and in
Value Moment 3, to generate and compare options. Which mechanisms recur, and whether
option generation is itself a distinct effect, are open questions this log exists to
investigate.

## Beta questions this log should answer

- Do other GMs notice new implications when mapping material they already know?
- Do the resulting insights change characters, relationships, or situations they intend
  to use?
- Which kinds of connections most often produce useful questions?
- How much of a campaign must be mapped before this value begins to appear?
- Does the value justify the effort required to create and maintain the map?
- Do users attribute the insight to the connected representation, or simply to spending
  additional time with the material?
- Do the insights produce material that actually reaches play?
- Does the process help new GMs, experienced GMs, or both — and in different ways?
- Does the option-generation value (VM3's pattern) appear for other GMs when adding new
  material, or mainly when adapting published material?
